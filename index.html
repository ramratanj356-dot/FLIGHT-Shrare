The ₹1,000-crore leak

A jet that takes off empty
is a flying loss.

Aviation runs on positioning: the aircraft has to be where the passenger is. The return trip, the ferry leg to a maintenance base, the aircraft going home after dropping a family in Goa — all of it flies with empty seats and a full fuel bill.

Operators absorb it and price it into the charter. Pilots and crew see the waste every week and have zero tools to recover a rupee of it. Travellers never even learn the seat existed. Everyone loses; only the fuel bill wins.

Straight answers

The questions everyone asks

Is selling seats on a repositioning flight even legal in India?

It depends on the structure, and we don't pretend otherwise. Commercial passenger transport in India requires an NSOP/charter permit held by the operator; pure seat resale on a charter is restricted. FLIGHT is being built around operator-approved manifests and cost-share structures reviewed by Indian aviation counsel before launch. If a structure can't be made compliant, we won't ship it.

Why would an operator give up 5–8% of a seat?

Because the alternative is 0%. The leg is already paid for in fuel, crew and handling. Any seat sold is margin that didn't exist an hour earlier, and FLIGHT handles demand, KYC, payment and refunds.

What happens if the flight gets rescheduled or cancelled?

Empty legs move — that's the nature of the product. Travellers are re-accommodated on another leg where possible, and otherwise refunded in full automatically from escrow. Fares are priced with that risk built in.

How is this different from a charter broker?

A broker sells you a whole aircraft and keeps a margin. FLIGHT is a two-sided marketplace: operators publish inventory themselves, and pricing is set by a model rather than a phone call. We don't own aircraft and we don't mark up the charter.

Does this actually make money?

At the calculator's defaults, one operator account generates about ₹73,900 a month in platform fees (8% of ₹9.24 lakh in seats). Forty operator accounts at that run rate is roughly ₹3.5 crore a year before ops costs. The full model, per-seat unit economics and first-100-customers plan are in BUSINESS-PLAN.md in this repo.

What about safety and security?

Every seat is on an aircraft flown by a licensed crew under an approved operator, with manifests filed and KYC completed before boarding. FLIGHT adds the paperwork layer; it never replaces the operator's legal responsibility for the flight.
